The author's deckbuilding principles, decoded

1. Match Catacombs' shape, don't combine everything

The whole point is to keep the experience feeling like Catacombs. The ratio targets (25% Companion / 20% Monster / 10% Device / 7.5% Gem / 37.5% Other / 27.5% Dragon) are reverse-engineered from base Catacombs. If you just shuffle every set together, those drift drastically.

2. Maximize unique cards, minimize duplicates

150 unique / 160 total = only 10 duplicates. Most Clank! sets ship with quantities (e.g. 2× Mercenary). The author kept singletons whenever possible — every card you pull from the Dungeon Row is more likely to be a new experience.

3. Cut OP cards, cut never-bought cards

Two sides of the same coin. If a card always wins the Dungeon Row, it dominates strategy and reduces variety. If it never gets bought, it's clogging the row. The author drops both.

4. Preserve Skill/Sword/Boots and Coin/Cost/Clank totals

Beyond type ratios, the resource economy ratios also matter. Too many sword-heavy cards make monsters trivial; too few make them impossible. The same goes for the noise (Clank!) levels — if the total Clank pumped into the bag per dungeon-row refresh shifts, the dragon's danger curve breaks.

5. Synergy clusters beat raw stats

Cards with named synergies (Companion-chain, Secret Tome, Gem, Lockpick, Prisoner, Monkey Idol, Crown, Artifact, etc.) are prioritized. A 1$ skill-1 card with a strong synergy slot beats a 1$ skill-2 generic.

6. Increase trash / draw / discard cards

These are the deck-builder mechanics that make Clank! a deck-builder rather than a glorified hand. Trash thins your starting chaff; draw and discard cycle you through your deck faster, so the cards you bought actually show up.

Cuts pattern analysis

Looking at the 25 cuts, three patterns emerge:

  • Duplicate-of-similar: Smoky Quartz / Fiery Quartz / Rose Quartz / Black Tourmaline / White Tourmaline are the 5 gems — author kept 3 (Rose, Black, White) and cut 2 (Smoky, Fiery) because they're functional duplicates.
  • Functional clones: Lightstick (3$/3 Skill/+1 Clank/1 VP) overlaps with Payoff (1$/3 Skill); Rebel General/Curator/Expert Guide are mid-cost companions with cheaper analogues elsewhere; Underground River/Darkened Alcove are 2-cost devices doing what cheaper cards do.
  • Niche/rarely-bought: Swindle (3$ for 1 VP + $3 — too expensive for its payoff), Skeleton (1-sword monster — trivial), Dusty Map (Promo) (situational tile manipulation).

What I tried and why it didn't yield more cards

  • Dire Wolf's official sites — Sunken Treasures / Mummy's Curse / Catacombs pages list only box contents (e.g. "40 dungeon cards"), not the individual cards. Their PDFs describe mechanics and tokens, not the card library.
  • BoardGameGeek — has community-compiled card lists in the Files section (e.g. "Clank! Card List" at filepage/152524), but BGG renders content via JavaScript and blocks anonymous scraping. The links exist; the content cannot be reached programmatically.
  • UltraBoardGames / RulesPal — have rules text and game flow, no card lists.
  • Tabletop Simulator Workshop mods — contain the actual card images as PNG sprites bundled with each mod. Extracting them requires subscribing in TTS, downloading the mod, and OCR'ing the images.
  • Reddit / forums — partial discussions, never full lists.

Bottom line: the author's transcription is genuinely the closest thing to a public, complete Clank! card database. To get the full ~275 cards he didn't select, you would need to either own each expansion or find the BGG file (the most likely source).

  • Use the TTS scripted mod — the "Clank! Catacombs + Full Expansion EXP(Scripted)" mod on Steam Workshop contains complete card data. If you can extract the deck JSON from the mod files, that's a programmatic complete list.
  • Stick with the author's 150. If his curation is good (and the numbers suggest it is), the missing cards are mostly the duplicates and weaker variants you'd cut anyway — see the cuts pattern in the Catacombs Cuts tab.
